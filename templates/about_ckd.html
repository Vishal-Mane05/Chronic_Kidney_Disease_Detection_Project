What is Chronic Kidney Disease?

A long-term progressive condition

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is a condition where the kidneys gradually lose their ability to filter waste products and excess fluids from the blood. It develops slowly over months or years, often without noticeable symptoms in the early stages.

Kidney Function

Healthy kidneys filter around 200 litres of blood daily, removing waste and maintaining fluid balance.

The Risk

If untreated, CKD can progress to kidney failure, requiring dialysis or transplant.

Good News

Early detection through lab tests and imaging can significantly slow progression.

Symptoms to Watch

CKD is often silent — these signs appear in later stages

Fatigue & Weakness
Shortness of Breath
Swollen Legs (Edema)
Frequent Urination
Loss of Appetite
Nausea & Vomiting
Difficulty Concentrating
Sleep Problems
Many people have no symptoms until CKD reaches an advanced stage. Regular blood and urine tests are the only way to detect it early.

Stages of CKD

Based on Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)

1
Normal Function

GFR ≥ 90
Mild kidney damage, normal filtering.

2
Mild Loss

GFR 60–89
Slightly reduced kidney function.

3
Moderate Damage

GFR 30–59
Noticeable decline, complications begin.

4
Severe Damage

GFR 15–29
Prepare for dialysis or transplant.

5
Kidney Failure

GFR < 15
Dialysis or kidney transplant required.

Prevention & Care

Lifestyle choices that protect your kidneys

Stay Hydrated
Healthy Diet
Regular Exercise
Control BP
Manage Sugar
Regular Checkups
Avoiding excess salt, processed foods, and NSAIDs significantly reduces kidney stress over time.

Treatment Options

CKD can be managed effectively with early action

CKD cannot always be fully cured, but progression can be significantly slowed with the right treatment plan tailored to the stage and underlying cause.

💊
Medication
Blood pressure drugs (ACE inhibitors), diabetes medication, and phosphate binders to manage complications.
🩺
Dialysis
Hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis artificially filters waste from blood when kidneys can no longer do so.
🫀
Kidney Transplant
Surgical replacement with a healthy donor kidney — the most effective long-term treatment for Stage 5 CKD.
